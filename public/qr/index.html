DARÉ · QR codes for the window vinyl

All of them point to the same address and nothing else, so they never need reprinting if a page moves. Each one was decoded back out of the rendered file to check it actually scans, including shrunk to 500px to stand in for a phone camera at a distance.

Use the SVG

Vinyl is cut and printed from vector, and a QR code is pure geometry, so it should never go to a printer as pixels. The SVGs open at 300mm square and scale from there with no loss. The PNGs are only for anyone who cannot take a vector.

How big

The rule is width = scanning distance ÷ 10.

1 metre100mm
2 metres200mm
3 metres300mm

On a window people scan from the pavement, so 300mm square is the minimum and bigger is better. At 300mm each module is about 8mm.

Four things that break it

  • Cropping the quiet zone. The clear border is part of the code. It is four modules wide and already in these files. Do not trim to the pattern.
  • Stretching it. Scale proportionally, always.
  • Putting the logo on it. Error correction is Q, so a quarter can be lost. Spend that on glass and dirt, not decoration.
  • Low contrast. Keep the dark part dark.

The transparent ones

A QR with no background takes the contrast of whatever is behind it, so on clear glass with the street showing through it will not scan. It needs a plain backing: a panel of cream or white vinyl under the black one, or of black under the cream one. If there is no backing, use one of the four above instead, where the background is part of the artwork.

Through glass

Test the real thing in daylight, standing where a passer-by would stand. Reflections are what kills a window code, so keep it below eye level rather than up in the glare, and off any part of the glass that mirrors the building opposite.
